✈️ Long flights

Insight & alternates

The climax of the original Red Rising trilogy — huge scale, relentless pacing, and the payoff for everything Darrow's been building toward. Long and propulsive enough to carry a full long-haul flight without running dry.

Want something different? If you finish early, Iron Gold (Red Rising #4) keeps the story going. For the same addictive "getting more powerful every chapter" feeling in much shorter books, try the Cradle series by Will Wight.

Insight & alternates

Achilles and Patroclus, retold — devastating, beautifully written, one of the most talked-about tragic romances in modern fiction. Worth starting fresh from page one even if you've read part of it before, since the ending only lands if the early relationship-building is fresh in your head.

Read this half of the flight home first — it's the heavier of the pair, so best to get the gut-punch done before Circe.

Want something different? Ithaca by Claire North or A Thousand Ships by Natalie Haynes are both strong Greek mythology retellings in a similar vein.

Insight & alternates

Greek mythology retold from the witch Circe's point of view — the Odyssey, Scylla, Icarus, all woven through her life. Ties in nicely with having just been in Greece, and Achilles appears later in the book with far more weight once you've read his story first.

Ends more hopeful and empowering than Song of Achilles — a good note to land the whole trip on.

Want something different? Read as a pair with Song of Achilles above; on its own, it's a strong standalone either way.

🚆 Trains & buses

Insight & alternates

An ordinary physics professor is abducted into a version of his life that isn't quite his own, and has to fight his way back to the family he knows. Thriller pacing with short, punchy chapters — built to survive being picked up and put down at every stop.

Want something different? Recursion, also by Crouch, is a loosely connected follow-up with the same mind-bending, unputdownable energy.

Insight & alternates

Gregor Samsa wakes up one morning transformed into a giant insect. Short, strange and dense — reads slower than the page count suggests, so best tackled in one sitting on a single leg rather than in fragments.

Want something different? The Stranger (Camus) or The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde (Stevenson) are similarly short, strange classics.

Insight & alternates

Two migrant ranch workers dream of saving enough to buy a small farm of their own during the Great Depression. Plain and simple on the surface, but the ending is genuinely devastating — best read solo on a quiet leg, not right before you need to be sociable.

Want something different? For something short but with a gentler (if still moving) touch, try A Man Called Ove by Fredrik Backman.

Insight & alternates

A fireman whose job is burning books starts questioning why. Short, but the prose is dense and almost poetic, so it reads slower than the page count implies — better suited to a longer train leg than a quick hop.

Want something different? Animal Farm (Orwell) or Brave New World (Huxley) are other short dystopian classics in the same spirit.

🏖️ Beach days

Insight & alternates

Two rival time-travelling agents on opposing sides of a war fall for each other through letters left hidden across timelines. Lush, poetic, epistolary prose — meant to be read slowly and savoured, not rushed between stops.

Want something different? Piranesi by Susanna Clarke has a similarly strange, atmospheric, one-sitting quality.

Insight & alternates

A found-family crew punches wormholes across the galaxy for a living. Episodic and character-driven rather than plot-driven — low stakes, warm, and easy to sink into for a long relaxed sit without worrying about losing the thread.

Want something different? If you want more of the universe afterwards, A Closed and Common Orbit follows different characters — no need to read the series in order.

Insight & alternates

Maggie wakes up inside the dark fantasy series she's been obsessively reading — and discovers that when she dies, she doesn't stay dead. Reviews flag a slow, info-heavy first third, so give it a long uninterrupted stretch to reach the payoff everyone says the second half delivers. Ends on a cliffhanger — book one of a series.

Want something different? If the slow burn isn't for you, Mistborn by Brandon Sanderson scratches a similar heist-fantasy itch with faster pacing.
